Disney Parks Industrial Engineering Case Study

Fall 2026
ForecastingCapacity planningOptimizationExcelTeam of 3

A three-part plan for a four-weekend fall festival, built for Walt Disney Parks & Resorts' Fall 2026 IE case with Aarnav Bhat and Justin Llamera. Each part feeds the next: how many guests will come, how many booths of each type they need, and how the festival should grow.

68,000visits forecast for 2026 (+21%)
45 / 21 / 27food, merchandise and activity booths
~6 minlongest wait on the busiest day
$55Kyearly gain from 12-hour days
  • Attendance forecast: averaged 5 trend models, then added 2026 factors (marketing, an anniversary, new markets, a concert). Identified a peak Saturday of about 10,800 guests as the planning target.
  • Booth mix: worked out the fewest booths needed to keep lines short at peak, then tested about 900 mixes for the remaining space. The winning mix fills all 120 spaces and earns the most (about $394,000 in vendor sales per day) without long lines.
  • Growth options: compared staying open later, adding Fridays and adding a fifth weekend. Staying open until 10 PM was the only option that makes money whether crowds are small or large, and the only one that shortens Saturday lunch lines.

Cimpress (Vistaprint) DCF Valuation

2026
ValuationDCFComparable companiesExcel

A discounted cash flow valuation of Cimpress, the parent of Vistaprint. I forecast revenue for each of its five business segments through fiscal 2030, turned that into free cash flow, and discounted it back to today.

5business segments forecast separately
10.3%discount rate (WACC)
6.7×exit multiple from 6 comparable companies
$4.4Bimplied enterprise value
  • Built the discount rate from the risk-free rate, the company's beta, its cost of debt and its debt/equity mix.
  • Set the exit value from the EV/EBITDA multiples of six printing and office-products peers, including Deluxe, Pitney Bowes and 4imprint.
  • Result: an implied equity value of about $3.0B, roughly $117 per share, compared with about $92 per share at the market value used in the model.

A neural network that predicts Microsoft's next-day stock price from 16 years of market data, tested on 2026 data it never saw. Switching from predicting prices to predicting daily returns cut the error by about a third.
