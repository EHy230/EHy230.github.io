Disney Parks Industrial Engineering Case Study

A three-part plan for a four-weekend fall festival, built for Walt Disney Parks & Resorts' Fall 2026 IE case with Aarnav Bhat and Justin Llamera: how many guests will come, how many booths of each type they need, and how the festival should grow.

Inside the Excel forecast model

Part 1 is built in four linked steps. Every number traces back to a case fact, a cited source or a labeled assumption, so changing one input updates the whole forecast.

2026 forecast from each trend method (history only)

With only five years of attendance (2021–2025), complex models like ARIMA would overfit. We fit five simple methods and averaged them. They agree within about 1,650 visits, so the real uncertainty is in the 2026 changes, not the trend.

Linear trend and ensemble (Excel)

=INTERCEPT(Attendance, t) + SLOPE(Attendance, t) * 6
=SUMPRODUCT(Forecasts, Include) / SUM(Include)

Hold-out error: forecasting 2024 and 2025 from earlier years

A rolling-origin backtest fit each method on 2021–2023 to forecast 2024, then on 2021–2024 to forecast 2025. Every method came in too low, which showed history alone understates this growing festival and justified adding 2026 drivers on top.

Error metric

APE  = ABS(Forecast - Actual) / Actual
MAPE = AVERAGE(APE_2024, APE_2025)

From the trend baseline to the 68,000-visit forecast

Four 2026 changes that history can't see were layered on top. Percentage effects scale the existing audience; new-market and concert guests are added as counts so nothing is double counted.

  • Marketing: +20% spend × 0.12 advertising elasticity (meta-analysis of 751 studies)
  • New markets: 700,000 new residents × local attendance rate × 8% distance-decay factor
  • Anniversary + concert: small labeled judgments (3% halo, 1,500 concert-only visits)

Forecast build-up

= Baseline * (1 + Spend * Elasticity) * (1 + Halo)
  + ReachVisits + ConcertVisits

1,000 simulated 2026 attendance totals

Each of the five uncertain drivers is drawn from a triangular distribution (Low / Expected / High), and the forecast is recomputed 1,000 times. Because drivers offset each other, the realistic planning range is much tighter than stacking every worst or best case.

Triangular sampling with RAND() (Excel)

=IF(U < (Mode-Min)/(Max-Min),
    Min + SQRT(U*(Max-Min)*(Mode-Min)),
    Max - SQRT((1-U)*(Max-Min)*(Max-Mode)))

98.9% of trials exceed 65,000 visits. Standard deviation: 1,489.

Details & files
  • Attendance forecast: averaged 5 trend models (with only 5 years of history, complex models would overfit), then added 2026 factors like marketing, new markets and a concert. A 1,000-run Monte Carlo simulation set the planning range at 66,300 to 70,300.
  • Booth mix: a queueing model found the fewest booths that keep every line short at peak, then we scored all ~900 possible mixes. The winner fills all 120 spaces and earns the most (about $394,000 in vendor sales per day) with no line longer than about 6 minutes.
  • Growth options: compared later hours, Fridays and a fifth weekend on cost, break-even and 5-year NPV. Staying open until 10 PM was the only option profitable in every demand scenario, and the only one that shortens Saturday lunch lines.

Loan Application Outcome Forecast (Absorbing Markov Chain)

Models how real bank loan applications move through 7 review steps until they are approved, denied or cancelled, using 154,152 status changes from the public BPI Challenge 2017 dataset. Trained on January–September 2016, then tested on applications from October–December that the model never saw.

154Kstatus changes used to train the model
3,167expected approvals from 5,049 open applications ($59.8M)
within 6%accuracy for applications already in review
63% vs. 46%predicted vs. actual approval rate overall
Markov chainsProbabilityBacktestingExcel
Details & files
  • Built the transition matrix from every status change, split it into moves between open steps and moves to final outcomes, and solved the fundamental matrix to get each application's odds of approval, denial or cancellation and its expected steps to finish. Built-in checks confirm every row of probabilities adds up.
  • Backtested on 4,951 applications still open 14 days after creation. Predictions were within about 6% for applications already in validation, but overall approvals came in 17 points lower than forecast.
  • Almost the whole gap came from applications stalled at the "Complete" step: the model predicted 1,825 approvals there and 975 happened. A Markov chain ignores how long an application has been waiting, so the recommendation is to recalibrate on recent data and account for time spent at each step.

Stock Price Forecasting with an LSTM

A PyTorch neural network trained on 16 years of market data and tested on 182 trading days of 2026 it never saw. Redesigning it to predict daily returns instead of prices cut the error by about a third, yet it only ties a simple "tomorrow = today" guess. That honest comparison is the main result.

2.4% → 1.6%average error, before and after the redesign
8engineered inputs, incl. RSI and market returns
182unseen 2026 trading days tested
49.5%up/down direction accuracy (coin flip)
PythonPyTorchTime seriesBacktesting
Details & files
  • Split data by date (train 2010–2024, validate 2025, test 2026), fitted all scaling on training data only, and used early stopping to avoid overfitting.
  • Direction accuracy of 49.5% vs. 48.9% for always guessing "up" shows daily moves are close to unpredictable from price history alone.
